A Storied Course

Nestled on the rolling heathland near Walton-on-the-Hill in Surrey, southwest of London, Walton Heath Golf Club is one of England's most celebrated inland courses. Founded in 1903, the club boasts two magnificent 18-hole championship courses — the Old and the New — both renowned for their heather-lined fairways, strategic bunkering, and expansive views across the Surrey countryside.

The Old Course was designed by Herbert Fowler, a former amateur cricketer who received his very first golf course commission here in 1902. The course opened for play in 1904, with three Open Championship winners — Harry Vardon, J.H. Taylor, and James Braid — hitting the ceremonial opening tee shots. No turf was used during the 16-month grow-in; every blade of grass was grown from seed.

Royal Connections

Championship-quality greens

Walton Heath's membership reads like a page from British history. Winston Churchill was a member from 1910 to 1965. King George VI held honorary membership. Most remarkably, the Duke of Windsor served as club captain in 1935 — and during his year as captain, he ascended to the throne as King Edward VIII.

This makes Walton Heath the only golf club in history to have had a reigning monarch as its captain. Despite these royal connections, the club was never granted a Royal Charter — one of golf's most intriguing ironies. Other notable members have included Prime Minister David Lloyd George.

Interesting Facts

46
Years

James Braid, winner of five Open Championships, served as club professional from 1904 until his death in 1950 — and famously refused to alter Herbert Fowler's original design despite having designed over 400 courses himself.

90+
Championships

Walton Heath has hosted over 90 major amateur and professional events, including the 1981 Ryder Cup, five European Opens, the 2023 Women's British Open, and 23 News of the World Match Play Championships.

1981
Ryder Cup

Walton Heath stepped in to host the Ryder Cup after construction at The Belfry wasn't completed in time. The United States team won 18½ to 9½ in what remains one of the competition's most memorable editions.

500
Acres

Cosmo Bonsor — MP, Bank of England director, and brother-in-law to Herbert Fowler's wife's sister — purchased 500 acres of heathland for the course, a family connection that gave birth to a masterpiece.

600
Early Members

Shortly after opening, the club surged to 600 members — 100 of them women, a progressive figure for the Edwardian era. A second nine holes was added in 1907, with the full New Course completed by 1913.

2005
US Open Qualifying

Since 2005, Walton Heath has hosted U.S. Open European qualifying. In the inaugural year, Michael Campbell qualified here and went on to win the U.S. Open at Pinehurst — the ultimate qualifier's fairy tale.

Traditions

Like many of Britain's finest private clubs, Walton Heath is a two-ball club where foursomes (alternate shot) is the preferred format. This tradition underscores the club's emphasis on pace of play, camaraderie, and the spirit of the game as it was meant to be played.

The Old Course has earned its place in the World's Top 100 every year since the rankings began in 1938 — a testament to Herbert Fowler's enduring vision and the club's meticulous stewardship of its heathland landscape. It is regularly compared with Sunningdale for the title of best 36 heathland holes in England.
