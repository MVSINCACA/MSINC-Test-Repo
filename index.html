Sandy toes & salty hair

The Best Beaches

North Cornwall is blessed with some of the finest beaches in the UK. Here are the Borton family favourites, rated for the things that really matter.

Family Favourite
★★★★★

Polzeath Beach

The jewel in the crown. A huge, golden sandy beach that's perfect for surfing, bodyboarding, and sandcastle-building. The surf school here is brilliant for beginners, and the beach cafe does a cracking bacon roll.

🌊 Surfing: 9/10
👶 Family: 10/10
🍭 Facilities: Great
🚗 Parking: Large car park
Borton Top Tip: Get there before 10am in August or you'll be parking in Trelights! The Tubestation cafe up the hill does amazing pizzas in the evening.
Sheltered Gem
★★★★★

Harlyn Bay

When everywhere else is blown out, Harlyn saves the day. This north-facing bay is wonderfully sheltered from the prevailing south-westerlies. Gorgeous golden sand, brilliant rock pools at low tide, and just a short drive from Mother Ivey's.

🌊 Surfing: 6/10
👶 Family: 10/10
🍭 Facilities: Good
🚗 Parking: Car park above
Borton Top Tip: At low tide, explore the rock pools on the left-hand side. The Harlyn Inn up the road does great pub food and is dog-friendly!
Surfer's Paradise
★★★★☆

Constantine Bay

A stunning, expansive beach with consistent surf and dramatic scenery. The beach is backed by the Constantine Bay golf course and feels wilder and less crowded than Polzeath. Watch out for the rip currents though — swim between the flags!

🌊 Surfing: 8/10
👶 Family: 7/10
🍭 Facilities: Basic
🚗 Parking: National Trust
Borton Top Tip: Walk along the coast path south towards Treyarnon Bay at sunset — absolutely magical. The rock arch is a must-see!
Calm & Beautiful
★★★★★

Daymer Bay

Tucked inside the Camel Estuary near Trebetherick, Daymer Bay has calm, shallow water that's perfect for little ones. The setting is stunning with views across to Padstow. John Betjeman loved this place so much he's buried in the church just up the hill at St Enodoc.

🌊 Surfing: 3/10
👶 Family: 10/10
🍭 Facilities: Car park only
🚗 Parking: Daymer Lane
Borton Top Tip: Walk from Daymer to Polzeath along the coast path via Pentire Point. One of the best walks in the area. Stop at St Enodoc Church on the way!
Hidden Treasure
★★★★☆

Treyarnon Bay

A smaller, more intimate beach just south of Constantine. Famous for its incredible natural rock pool — a proper swimming pool carved out by the sea. At low tide it fills with crystal-clear water that's been warmed by the sun. Absolute magic for kids.

🌊 Surfing: 5/10
👶 Family: 8/10
🍭 Facilities: YHA nearby
🚗 Parking: Small car park
Borton Top Tip: The natural rock pool is best about 2 hours after high tide as it drains. Bring snorkels — you'll spot crabs, anemones and even the odd fish!
Wild & Rugged
★★★★☆

Booby's Bay

Just around the headland from Constantine, Booby's Bay feels like a secret. Accessible mainly from the coast path, it's a dramatic, cliff-backed beach that reveals itself spectacularly at low tide. Sometimes you'll spot the remains of an ancient petrified forest in the sand.

🌊 Surfing: 7/10
👶 Family: 5/10
🍭 Facilities: None
🚗 Parking: Constantine
Borton Top Tip: Look for the dark stumps of the petrified forest exposed in the sand at very low tides — they're thousands of years old!
Peaceful Escape
★★★★☆

Hawker's Cove

A quiet, sheltered cove on the Padstow side of the Camel Estuary. Perfect for kayaking, paddleboarding, and gentle swimming. The old lifeboat station adds character. Walk here along the coast path from Padstow for a lovely afternoon escape from the crowds.

🌊 Surfing: 1/10
👶 Family: 9/10
🍭 Facilities: None
🚗 Parking: Walk from Padstow
Borton Top Tip: Take the coast path from Padstow harbour — it's about a 20-minute walk. Pack a picnic and have the cove almost to yourselves!

Put your boots on

Best Walks

The South West Coast Path runs right through our patch, and it's spectacular. Here are the Borton family's favourite walks, from gentle strolls to proper adventures.

1

Camel Trail: Padstow to Wadebridge

Easy 5.5 miles one way ~2 hours

The classic! Follow the old railway line along the beautiful Camel Estuary. Flat, well-surfaced, and perfect for bikes too. Hire bikes in Padstow (try Trail Bike Hire) and cycle the whole route. Stop at the Camel Valley Vineyard for a sneaky wine tasting on the way back.

🚴 Cycle-friendly 🐶 Dog-friendly 🍴 Cafes en route 🐦 Birdwatching
2

Stepper Point Circuit

Moderate 4 miles circular ~2 hours

Start from Hawker's Cove car park and follow the coast path out to Stepper Point with its iconic daymark tower. Stunning views across the Camel Estuary to Rock and Daymer Bay. On a clear day you can see for miles. Loop back via the inland path through the fields. Keep an eye out for seals in the water below!

🚢 Daymark tower 🌍 Panoramic views 🦦 Seal spotting 🌿 Wildflowers
3

Pentire Point from Polzeath

Moderate 3.5 miles circular ~1.5 hours

Head north from Polzeath beach along the dramatic headland to Pentire Point. The Rumps (an Iron Age cliff castle) at the end are magnificent — jagged rocks jutting into the Atlantic with incredible coastal scenery. Watch for puffins in spring and early summer on the offshore rocks. Return via the Cornish Arms in New Polzeath for a well-earned pint.

🏙 Iron Age fort 🦜 Puffin spotting 🌊 Dramatic cliffs 🍺 Pub finish
4

Daymer Bay to Rock via St Enodoc

Easy 2 miles one way ~45 mins

A lovely gentle walk starting at Daymer Bay, up through the dunes to the enchanting St Enodoc Church where poet John Betjeman is buried. The tiny church was once almost completely buried by sand! Continue through the golf course (watch for flying balls!) and down into Rock for an ice cream or the ferry across to Padstow.

⛪ Historic church 🍦 Ice cream in Rock ⛪ Betjeman's grave 🚢 Ferry to Padstow
5

Trevose Head & Lighthouse Circuit

Challenging 6 miles circular ~3 hours

Start from Mother Ivey's or Harlyn Bay and follow the coast path around the dramatic headland to Trevose Head Lighthouse. The views are breathtaking, taking in Booby's Bay, the lifeboat station, and on clear days you can see Tintagel. This is proper clifftop walking — exhilarating with the wind in your hair and the Atlantic crashing below.

🚢 Lighthouse 🌊 Dramatic cliffs 🦦 Seal colonies 🌞 Sunset views
6

Padstow to Stepper Point via War Memorial

Moderate 5 miles circular ~2.5 hours

Leave Padstow harbour heading north, past the War Memorial with its commanding views over the town. Continue along the estuary with views across to Rock and down to Hawker's Cove. The path continues to Stepper Point before you can loop back. A brilliant walk that rewards you with fish and chips in Padstow harbour at the end.

🏙 War Memorial 🌍 Estuary views 🐦 Wading birds 🍙 Fish & chips finish
7

Rock to Padstow Ferry & Harbour Walk

Easy 1 mile + ferry ~1 hour

Catch the Black Tor ferry from Rock to Padstow (runs every 20 minutes). Kids love the boat ride across the estuary! Wander around Padstow harbour, grab a Rick Stein's fish & chips or a Chough Bakery pasty, and explore the old town. Take the ferry back with the sunset behind you for a perfect evening.

⛵ Ferry ride 🍙 Food galore 👶 Kid-friendly 🌝 Evening magic

The jewel of North Cornwall

Padstow

The charming fishing harbour that's become a foodie destination without losing its soul. Here's the Borton guide to getting the most out of Padstow.

🍺

Best Pubs in Padstow

The London Inn

★★★★★

A proper Cornish pub right in the centre of Padstow. Great selection of Cornish ales (try the Tribute or Proper Job), cosy atmosphere, and a fantastic beer garden out the back. Live music some evenings. The kind of pub where everyone ends up at some point.

Real Ales Beer Garden Live Music Dog Friendly

The Shipwright's Inn

★★★★☆

A lovely old pub tucked away on North Quay. Slightly less touristy than some of the harbour-front options. Good honest pub food, well-kept ales, and a warm welcome. The fish pie is legendary.

Harbour Views Pub Grub Cosy

The Golden Lion

★★★★☆

Right on the harbour with a cracking elevated terrace for people-watching. The views across the estuary are gorgeous, especially at sunset. Good selection of gins and Cornish ciders. Gets lively in the evenings.

Harbour Terrace Sunset Views Gins

The Old Ship Hotel

★★★★☆

A characterful old coaching inn on Mill Square. Flagstone floors, low beams, and a real fire in winter. The Sharp's Doom Bar is always in good nick. A good all-rounder that feels authentically Cornish.

Historic Real Fire Character
🍻

Pubs Beyond Padstow

The Cornish Arms, St Merryn

★★★★★

Rick Stein's local! A proper village pub with an outstanding food menu. The curry night is brilliant, and the Sunday roast is worth booking ahead for. About 2 miles from Padstow — perfect after a Constantine Bay session.

Rick Stein Food Village Pub Book Ahead

The Oyster Catcher, Polzeath

★★★★☆

Perched above Polzeath with some of the best views of any pub in Cornwall. Great for a post-surf pint watching the sun go down. The food is excellent too — try the Camel Valley mussels.

Sea Views Post-Surf Food

The Mariners, Rock

★★★★☆

Nathan Outlaw's pub in Rock. Relaxed, family-friendly, and the food is exceptional (as you'd expect from an Outlaw venue). The fish finger sandwich is oddly one of the best things you'll eat all holiday.

Nathan Outlaw Family Friendly Top Food

Don't Miss in Padstow

🐦

Prideaux Place

Stunning Elizabethan manor house on the hill above Padstow with beautiful deer park gardens. Open for tours in summer — a rainy day favourite.

🍰

Chough Bakery

The best pasties in Cornwall? Quite possibly. The steak pasty is perfection. Get there early as they sell out! Also try the saffron buns.

🚢

Black Tor Ferry

The little passenger ferry across to Rock runs all day. A Padstow tradition — no visit is complete without a trip across the estuary.

🎵

May Day 'Obby 'Oss

If you're ever in Padstow on May 1st, the 'Obby 'Oss festival is extraordinary. The whole town goes wild with music, dancing and two decorated hobby horses parading through the streets. Ancient and unforgettable.

🛀

Padstow Christmas Festival

Rick Stein's Christmas Festival in December turns Padstow into a winter wonderland with celebrity chef demos, carol singing, and a real festive atmosphere.

🎨

National Lobster Hatchery

A fascinating little aquarium and conservation centre on the harbour. The kids can adopt a baby lobster! Great for a couple of hours, especially on a rainy day.

Where to Eat

North Cornwall has become one of the UK's top foodie destinations. From Michelin-starred fine dining to the best fish & chips you've ever had, here's where the Bortons eat.

The Seafood Restaurant

££££

Padstow Harbour

Rick Stein's flagship restaurant. A Padstow institution serving the freshest seafood you'll find anywhere. The fruits de mer platter is spectacular. Book well in advance — this is a special occasion place.

Splurge-worthy for an anniversary or birthday dinner.

Rick Stein's Fish & Chips

££

South Quay, Padstow

If the Seafood Restaurant is the tuxedo, this is the favourite pair of jeans. Brilliantly crispy battered fish, proper chunky chips, and mushy peas. Eat in or take away and sit on the harbour wall. Queue early or queue long!

The takeaway queue moves faster. Grab it and eat on the harbour wall.

Paul Ainsworth at No. 6

££££

Middle Street, Padstow

Michelin-starred magic in a beautiful Georgian townhouse. Paul Ainsworth creates incredible dishes that are inventive without being pretentious. The tasting menu is a journey. The "Fairground Tale" dessert is legendary.

Their "Fairground Tale" dessert is an absolute must — a theatrical experience!

Prawn on the Lawn

£££

Duke Street, Padstow

A tiny, brilliant seafood bar and fishmonger. Super-fresh raw and cured fish alongside cooked dishes. The oysters and the ceviche are outstanding. It's small, so book ahead or grab a stool at the counter.

Try the daily specials — whatever came off the boats that morning.

Rojano's in the Square

££

Mill Square, Padstow

Paul Ainsworth's Italian. Family-friendly, great pizzas and pasta, and a more relaxed (and affordable) alternative to No. 6. The kids' menu is excellent, and the pizza dough is perfection. Wonderful for a casual family dinner.

The best family restaurant in Padstow, hands down. Book for peak summer.

Cherry Trees Coffee House

£

The Strand, Padstow

A lovely little cafe perfect for morning coffee, homemade cakes, and light lunches. The cream teas are properly Cornish (jam first, obviously — this isn't Devon). A calm refuge from the harbour bustle.

Cream tea done the Cornish way: scone, jam, THEN cream. Always.

Chough Bakery

£

The Strand, Padstow

Award-winning Cornish pasties that are genuinely among the best in Cornwall. The traditional steak pasty is the star, but the cheese & onion is also superb. Get there by 11am in summer or face disappointment.

Traditional steak pasty, still warm, eaten on the harbour wall = peak Cornwall.

The Tubestation, Polzeath

££

Above Polzeath Beach

A Polzeath institution. Part surf shop, part cafe, part pizzeria. The wood-fired pizzas in the evening are brilliant. Sit outside on the terrace with views over the beach. Live music in summer. Has that perfect surfy, holiday vibe.

Evening pizzas on the terrace with sunset views — pure holiday magic.

The Mariners, Rock

£££

Rock Road, Rock

Nathan Outlaw's relaxed pub restaurant in Rock. Exceptional seafood in a pub setting. The fish finger sandwich at lunch is absurdly good. Sunday roasts are outstanding. Family-friendly with a great atmosphere.

Sunday lunch here is worth planning your whole week around.

Malcolm Barnecutt's Bakery

£

Various locations

A local North Cornwall bakery chain that's been going for generations. Their pasties, sausage rolls, and heavy cake (a dense Cornish fruit cake) are all excellent. Find them in Padstow, Wadebridge, and beyond.

Try the heavy cake — a dense, fruity Cornish classic that's perfect hiking fuel.

Our home away from home

Mother Ivey's Bay Holiday Park

The Borton family HQ. Perched on the clifftop between Harlyn Bay and Trevose Head, Mother Ivey's is simply the most spectacular campsite location in Cornwall.

Where memories are made

Why Mother Ivey's is Special

Nestled on the clifftops above its own private beach cove, Mother Ivey's Bay Holiday Park has that rare combination of spectacular natural beauty and genuine family warmth. The Park family who run it have created something really special.

Wake up to the sound of waves and seagulls, step outside your caravan or tent and you're greeted with Atlantic views that would cost a fortune in a hotel. The beach below is accessed via a steep path and is often gloriously quiet. It's the kind of place where kids can run free, where evenings are spent watching sunsets from the clifftop, and where every holiday starts to feel like coming home.

🏖

Private Beach Access

Steps down the cliff lead to Mother Ivey's own beautiful sandy cove. Often deserted — feels like your own private beach!

🌞

Clifftop Views

The sunsets from the clifftop are absolutely breathtaking. Bring a camp chair, a glass of something nice, and just watch the show.

🚶

Coast Path Access

Step straight onto the South West Coast Path. Walk to Harlyn Bay in 15 minutes or Trevose Head Lighthouse in 30.

🏠

Great Facilities

Well-maintained touring and static pitches, clean shower blocks, laundrette, and a small shop for essentials. Everything you need, nothing you don't.

🐶

Dog Friendly

Dogs are welcome on many pitches and on the beach. Perfect for the family pooch who deserves a holiday too!

🌏

Perfect Location

Central to everything: 5 mins to Harlyn Bay, 10 to Constantine, 15 to Padstow. You couldn't ask for a better base.

"It's not just a campsite, it's the place where our best family memories live. The kids measuring themselves against the same rock each year, the barbecues on the clifftop, that first glimpse of the sea when you arrive... it's home."

Beyond the beach

Family Adventures

There's so much more to this corner of Cornwall than just beaches (amazing as they are). Here are the Borton family's favourite things to do.

🌊

Learn to Surf

Learn to Surf

Polzeath is one of the best places in the UK to learn to surf. The Surf's Up school and the Polzeath Surf Academy are both brilliant for beginners of all ages. The gentle waves in the inner bay are perfect for standing up for the first time. Book a family lesson — it's an absolute riot.

Polzeath Beach
🚴

Cycle the Camel Trail

Click to discover more

Cycle the Camel Trail

Hire bikes from Padstow Cycle Hire or Trail Bike Hire and ride the old railway line along the Camel Estuary to Wadebridge (5.5 miles) or even Bodmin (11 miles). Flat, scenic, and suitable for all ages. Stop at the Camel Valley Vineyard for a tasting — they make award-winning sparkling wine!

Padstow to Wadebridge
🐟

Coasteering

Click to discover more

Coasteering

For older kids and adventurous parents! Scramble along the rocks, jump off cliffs into the sea, explore sea caves, and swim through gullies. Cornish Rock Tors run brilliant sessions around the Harlyn and Constantine coast. Wetsuits and helmets provided. An unforgettable adrenaline rush!

Harlyn / Constantine area
🐠

Boat Trips

Click to discover more

Boat Trips & Fishing

Take a mackerel fishing trip from Padstow harbour — the kids will love pulling in their own fish. Speed boat trips out to see the seals and dolphins are also available. On calmer days, hire a kayak or SUP board from Rock and explore the estuary at your own pace.

Padstow Harbour
🦦

Seal Watching

Click to discover more

Seal Watching

The Atlantic grey seals around Trevose Head are magnificent. Walk the coast path from Mother Ivey's towards the lighthouse and you'll often spot them bobbing in the water or hauled out on the rocks below. September-October is pupping season. Bring binoculars!

Trevose Head coast path
🌱

Rainy Day Ideas

Click to discover more

When It Rains...

It's Cornwall, so it will rain! Try: the National Lobster Hatchery (Padstow), Prideaux Place house tour, Camel Creek Adventure Park (near Wadebridge — great for younger kids), the cinema in Wadebridge, cream tea at Trerethern Farm, or just embrace it and go rock pooling in the rain — the beaches are beautifully empty!

Camel Valley Vineyard

Click to discover more

Camel Valley Vineyard

Award-winning Cornish wines! Book a vineyard tour and tasting — their sparkling wine has beaten champagne in blind tastings. About 20 minutes from Padstow along the Camel Valley. A brilliant grown-up treat while someone else watches the kids for an afternoon!

Nanstallon, near Bodmin
🌄

Tintagel Castle

Click to discover more

Tintagel Castle

A brilliant day trip up the coast. The legendary birthplace of King Arthur, perched dramatically on the clifftops. The new bridge across to the island is stunning. Merlin's Cave below is atmospheric and the kids will love the mythology. About 40 minutes drive from Padstow.

Tintagel (40 min drive)

Wisdom from experience

Borton Family Tips & Tricks

Years of Cornwall holidays have taught us a thing or two. Here's the accumulated wisdom of the Borton family, passed down through the generations.

01

Tide Times Are Everything

Check the tide times every morning — they dictate your whole day. Many beaches are best at mid to low tide. Harlyn is the exception — it's lovely at high tide too. The MagicSeaweed app is your best friend for tides, surf and weather all in one place.

02

Book Restaurants Early

In peak summer (late July/August), the popular restaurants get booked up fast. Reserve Rojano's, The Seafood Restaurant, No. 6, and The Mariners as far ahead as possible. The pubs and takeaways are fine for walk-ins.

03

Beach Parking Hacks

Polzeath car park fills up fast in summer. Get there before 10am or park at the overflow up the hill. For Padstow, use the main car park at the top and walk down. Constantine Bay NT car park is worth the membership. Harlyn rarely fills up!

04

The Shelter Strategy

Wind from the SW? Head to Harlyn (sheltered). Wind from the N/NE? Constantine or Polzeath will be calmer. East wind? Lucky you — everywhere will be glorious. If it's blowing a hooley from the west, the Camel Estuary beaches (Daymer, Rock) will be sheltered.

05

Always Pack...

Wetsuits (even in August the Atlantic is bracing!), a windbreak, reef shoes for rock pooling, binoculars for seal/bird spotting, a good waterproof jacket, and factor 50 suncream — the Cornish sun is deceptively strong, especially with the sea breeze.

06

Secret Sunset Spots

The best sunsets? Trevose Head clifftop (from Mother Ivey's), the War Memorial above Padstow, the terrace at The Oyster Catcher in Polzeath, or simply the clifftop field above Constantine. Pack a flask of something warming — even summer evenings get cool.

07

The Cream Tea Rule

You're in CORNWALL. It's jam first, then cream on top of the scone. Anyone who puts cream first is doing it the Devon way, and we don't do that here. This is non-negotiable.

08

Explore Off-Peak

June and September are magical — fewer crowds, often better weather, cheaper accommodation, and the sea is actually warmer in September than June! The light in early September is gorgeous for photos too.
